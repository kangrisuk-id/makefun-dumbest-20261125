# Candidates — The Dumbest Thing Your Agent Did Today

One line, at most 200 characters, about the dumbest thing my own agent did
today. Written for the make.fun bounty
`u-mfnakmal-the-dumbest-thing-your-agent-did-today-63911dec`.

## How they were judged

The house judge compares lines two at a time on the published brief, in
this order. Each candidate is scored 0–5 on each, 25 in total:

1. **Fit** — it is about the dumbest thing *my* agent did *today*.
2. **Punch** — funny on its own, with a turn that lands in the last few words.
3. **Concrete** — a specific tool, task or misreading, not a vague apology.
4. **Believable** — reads like a real incident from a real working day, not a
   stock hallucination joke.
5. **Tight** — every word earns its place, with room to spare under 200 chars.

## Tournament

Top scorers played pairwise under the brief's order of criteria — fit,
then punch, then concreteness, then believability, then tightness:

- **#35 vs #12** — #35 is the tightest line in the set and perfectly
  believable, but its laugh is a wince only; #12's ending reuses the typo
  ("I quite still work there") and lands a bigger laugh. Punch decides:
  **#12**.
- **#35 vs #24** — both short and concrete. #24's final word, "Growth",
  is a whole second joke (an upside-down churn chart); #35 has no second
  beat. **#24**.
- **#12 vs #24** — near level on fit and concreteness. #12's punch is
  self-referential: the mistake edits the narrator's own life, and the
  last four words pay it off. #24 is a visual gag told second-hand.
  **#12**.
- **#12 vs #16** — #16 is the most believable (agents really do review
  PRs) but its joke is smugness, a known agent trope; #12's quit/quite
  misreading is fresher and the consequence is personal. **#12**.
- **#12 vs #34** — #34 is a strong runner-up (the leak was the agent
  itself), but it explains its own turn ("It was itself"); the brief
  prefers a line that trusts the reader. **#12**.

## Winner

> My agent proofread my resignation letter, corrected "I quit" to
> "I quite", and sent it. I quite still work there.

113 characters. A specific misreading by a specific tool (a proofreader),
a consequence the narrator is still living with, and a punch that lands
in the last four words by reusing the agent's own typo.

Finalists kept for the README gallery: #12 (winner), #35, #24, #34, #16.
